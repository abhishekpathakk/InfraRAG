# ZoektPushIndexingLagHigh

## Overview

More than **10%** of Zoekt push indexing tasks (`index_repo`) have taken more
than **5 minutes** from their scheduled time to completion, and this has held
for an hour. New commits are slow to become searchable in exact code search.
Search itself keeps serving the existing index, so results are only missing
recent changes.

This catches indexing lag in the range of minutes to an hour.
[ZoektTaskQueueNotDraining](./ZoektTaskQueueNotDraining.md) and the
`zoekt_tasks` apdex SLO only react once tasks approach the 2-hour
`APDEX_THRESHOLD_S` deadline.

## Alert behavior

The source is the `gitlab_search_zoekt_task_duration_seconds` histogram,
recorded by Rails when a node reports a task done
([`ee/lib/gitlab/metrics/zoekt_tasks_slis.rb`](https://gitlab.com/gitlab-org/gitlab/-/blob/master/ee/lib/gitlab/metrics/zoekt_tasks_slis.rb)).
It measures the time between a task's `perform_at` and its completion.

The rule computes, over a 30-minute window, the share of `index_repo` tasks
outside the `le="300"` bucket. `300` is an exact `DURATION_BUCKETS` edge, so no
quantile interpolation is involved. It fires when that share is above 10% for
an hour, and only while at least one `index_repo` task per second completes, so
a quiet period cannot fire on a handful of slow tasks.

The rule is fleet-wide rather than per node: lag usually comes from shared
task pickup, and `ZoektTaskQueueNotDraining` already names a node that is
falling behind.

### How the threshold was chosen

Backtested against gprd from 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-22:

| Rule | Firings in 7 days |
| --- | --- |
| Share over 5 minutes > 5%, for 30m | 15 |
| Share over 5 minutes > 10%, for 30m | 9 |
| **Share over 5 minutes > 10%, for 1h (this rule)** | **4** |
| Share over 5 minutes > 20%, for 1h | 0 |

The four firings were all during measured indexing degradations: one on
2026-10-16, and three during a system-wide force reindex burst on 2026-10-21.
Ordinary push bursts did not fire.

Severity **s4**, non-paging, `user_impacting: no`.

## First response

1. **Open the
   [observability dashboard](https://dashboards.gitlab.net/d/zoekt-observability/zoekt3a-zoekt-observability-orr)**
   and look at the *Push indexing tasks waiting over 5 minutes* panel, next to
   *Task processing queue size*. Check whether one node or the whole fleet is
   behind.

1. **Check the task mix.** In the *Indexing request rate* panel, a jump in
   `force_index_repo` or `delete_repo` usually means a system-wide event: a
   schema-version reindex, a node eviction or a rebalancing. Those drain on
   their own; watch that the share trends down.

1. **Check whether one root namespace dominates.** In Kibana, count
   `Search::Zoekt::IndexingTaskWorker` jobs with `json.job_status: done` in
   `pubsub-sidekiq-inf-gprd*`, grouped by `json.meta.root_namespace`. One
   namespace holding a large share points at a single heavy actor.

1. **No action is a valid outcome** when the cause is a known system-wide
   reindex and the share is falling.

## Escalation

Not a paging alert. It is posted to `#g_global_search_alerts` through the
`global_search` team label. If lag keeps growing, or one namespace dominates,
raise it with the Global Search team.
